**SUBJECT:** DEMAND FOR REFUND OF RS. 1,45,000/- AND FREE WARRANTY REPLACEMENT OF DEFECTIVE ELECTRIC SCOOTER (INVOICE #VR-9981) IN VIOLATION OF CONSUMER PROTECTION ACT, 2019, SECTION 74 INDIAN CONTRACT ACT, 1872, AND NOTICE OF INTENTION TO INSTITUTE PROCEEDINGS BEFORE THE DISTRICT CONSUMER DISPUTES REDRESSAL COMMISSION, LUCKNOW

1. **FACTS OF THE CASE AND CAUSE OF ACTION:**
   The undersigned, Vikram Singh, is a bona fide consumer residing in Lucknow. On 10 September, the undersigned purchased an electric scooter from the Opposite Party, VoltRide Motors Pvt Ltd (Hazratganj, Lucknow), bearing Invoice Number VR-9981, for a single upfront purchase price of Rs. 1,45,000/- (Rupees One Lakh Forty-Five Thousand Only). The said purchase was supported by a formal Tax Invoice and a corresponding payment receipt. 

2. **DEFECT AND DEFICIENCY IN SERVICE:**
   Within a mere 15 days of the aforementioned transaction, specifically around 25 September, the battery of the said electric scooter failed completely, rendering the vehicle entirely non-operational. The rapid manifestation of this defect within 15 days of purchase establishes a clear manufacturing and/or latent defect in the product. 

3. **REFUSAL TO HONOR WARRANTY AND UNFAIR TRADE PRACTICE:**
   Upon reporting the defect to the Opposite Party’s service center, the service center explicitly refused to honor the warranty replacement. Instead of providing a free replacement under the warranty terms, the service center arbitrarily demanded a sum of Rs. 60,000/- (Rupees Sixty Thousand Only) from the undersigned for a new battery pack. 

4. **FINANCIAL CLAIM AND DOCTRINAL BASIS:**
   The undersigned’s primary claim is for a full refund of the original purchase price of Rs. 1,45,000/- (Rupees One Lakh Forty-Five Thousand Only), or alternatively, a free replacement of the defective unit. Under Section 2(10) and 2(11) of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, the dead battery within 15 days constitutes a 'defect' and a 'deficiency in service'. The unilateral refusal to replace the defective battery within the warranty period entitles the consumer to a full refund or free replacement. The Rs. 60,000/- quoted by the service center for a new battery pack is entirely irrelevant to the consumer's primary claim for the defective unit and cannot be set off against the original purchase price of Rs. 1,45,000/-. Furthermore, under Section 2(47) of the CPA 2019, demanding Rs. 60,000/- for a warranty-covered defect constitutes an Unfair Trade Practice. As per the principles laid down in *Pioneer Urban Land & Infrastructure Ltd. vs. Govindan Raghavan (2019) 5 SCC 725*, one-sided, unfair clauses in standard-form consumer contracts, such as arbitrary refusal to honor warranties, constitute an Unfair Trade Practice under the Consumer Protection Act. Additionally, the arbitrary demand of Rs. 60,000/- is an unconscionable penalty attempt under Section 74 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, which is legally unenforceable without proof of actual loss.

5. **EVIDENTIARY BASIS:**
